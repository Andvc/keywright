# Keywright: the world

The world exists to explain the game. Every mechanic should have a reason you could tell a stranger in a tavern, and
every reason should make you want to open one more chest. This is the reference for anything that speaks, sells, falls
out of a chest or happens in a scene; `docs/design.md` has the rules and the numbers.

## The premise in four answers

**Why is the world full of chests?** Three hundred years ago the demon of avarice, **Avarax, the Gilded Maw**, ruled
the realm of Aldmark through its treasure: every loose coin was his eye, every unsheathed blade his hand. King **Aldric
the Wise** and the archmages of the **Keyhold** worked **the Locking**: every treasure in the realm, from a farmer's
savings to the crown jewels, was shut into a warded chest. Blind and starving, the Maw was chained beneath the capital
behind the **Gilded Gate**. The chests never went away. Three centuries on they're everywhere: in attics and
barrows, on old battlefields, in goblin warrens, abbey crypts and noble strongrooms. Folk trade them unopened, the way
they'd trade a cow.

**Why sell them instead of opening them?** A Locking chest can't be seen into, scried or smashed: the ward turns any
blade and any spell. It opens for one kind of person only, a **Keywright** (someone born with the Key-mark), and only
once the chest is rightfully theirs: bought fairly or freely given, never stolen. So everyone else sells. The ward lets
one thing through: sound. Everybody shakes a chest before they sell it, and everybody guesses.

**Why can you open them?** You're one of Bram's kin, and the Key-mark runs in the family. Uncle Bram opened chests at
the Brindle crossroads for forty years until his mark faded with age; now it shows on your hand, and he hands you his
stall. You buy a chest, it's yours, the ward lets go, and you pick the old lock underneath.

**What does opening them accomplish?** For the folk who sell to you: coin in a world that's short of it. For the realm:
treasure back in the light, three hundred years late. For the Maw: every chest opened loosens the Locking a little.
That's what nobody tells you at first. The **Gilded Guild**, which licenses Keywrights and takes a weekly tithe "to keep
the Gate", wants you opening chests as fast as you can, and wants its tithe in gold. Gold you can see.

## The places (the shop's tiers)

Progress is a journey inward, from the edge of the realm to the Gate at its heart. Each move brings new people, new
things to hear, and new trouble.

| Tier | Place | Who comes | New to hear and do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brindle Crossroads**, Bram's stall where the north road meets the river | villagers, an innkeeper, young adventurers back from the Barrow | coin, weapons, cloaks; the three strengths; haggling, naming a price |
| 2 | **Thornwick Market**, a market town on the edge of the Old Wood | a goblin, a huntress, a druid, a priest, a smuggler | potions and spells; locks; false bottoms and the drop; the display shelf |
| 3 | **A shop in Crownhold**, the capital | an archmage and his apprentice, a dwarf miner, a noblewoman, a knight, a pawnbroker, a mudlark | gems; two or three of a kind; sand; enchantment and the witchlight; the shop's name (synergies) |
| 4 | **The Royal Emporium**, in the palace's shadow, by the Gilded Gate | demons through the cracking Gate, a court lady, the King in disguise, and once in a while the Maw himself | demon gold (iron: the lodestone silences it); hurried, greedy and bargaining customers |

Each move costs the renovation's price: "Move to Thornwick", "Move to Crownhold", "The Royal Emporium".

## The people of the story

- **Uncle Bram**: an old Keywright whose mark has faded. Warm, proud, a little ashamed of the debt. Your mentor.
- **Mister Vane**: Collector of the **Gilded Guild**. Polite, pale, patient, and the Maw's.
- **Mara, the Hooded Woman**: the last archmage of the Keyhold. She's been hunting the Aldric Key's shards for twenty
  years.
- **Nim the Peddler**: a horned, cheerful half-something who sells **curios**, oddments that come out of Locking chests
  and bend the rules of the trade. Nobody asks what Nim is.
- **The Stranger**: brings a chest wrapped in iron chains, so nothing inside can move. Never says where from.
- **King Aldric IX**: old, tired, worried about the Gate. Calls himself Old Aldo, and sells you chests from the Royal
  Treasury to see if you're the Keywright the realm needs. When the Key is whole he brings you the Crown Chest himself,
  crowned.
- **Avarax, the Gilded Maw**: the demon of greed. Weak enough to need the Guild, strong enough to visit the Emporium in
  a borrowed body dripping with rings.

## Things (the kinds)

Six kinds of things, each with the sound you'd expect from it and the strength of shake it takes to stir it. The world
made them; the rules didn't change. Since 0.17 every one of them is something you'd want to find: the commonest coin is
grave silver from the Barrow, the plainest weapon a goblin's cleaver, and nobody locks away a letter. (In code the kinds
are still Arms, Garb and Scrolls, so saves carry over.)

| Kind | Sound | Stirs at | Notes | Common to legendary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coin | jingle | gentle | delicate | Barrow Silver, Moonsilver Marks (elven), Dwarf-Gold Crowns, Sunken Sovereigns (from the town under the Aldwater), Dragon Gold |
| Weapons | clank | firm | loud; iron (the lodestone holds it) | Goblin Cleaver, Dwarven Axe, Warden's Blade (the Wardens carried the Locking's chests), Flamebrand, Dragonslayer |
| Cloaks | flump | hard | soft | Wolfskin Cloak, Elven Cloak, Cloak of Shadows, Archmage's Mantle, Phoenix Mantle |
| Potions | slosh | gentle | delicate; glass, breaks if dropped (from Thornwick) | Healing Draught, Elixir of Vigour, Invisibility Draught, Mermaid's Breath, Ambrosia |
| Spells | rustle | hard | soft: scrolls and spellbooks (from Thornwick) | Scroll of Sparks, Scroll of Fireball, Summoner's Grimoire, Lich's Tome, Keyhold Codex (the archmages' own) |
| Gems | tinkle | gentle | delicate (from Crownhold) | Moonstone, Elfstone, Blood Ruby, Starstone, Dragon's Eye |

Fakes: **Demon Gold** (gilded iron coins and gems: the lodestone silences them), **Paste Gems**, and **scroll cases
full of sand** (sand pours at a gentle shake; parchment only rustles at a hard one). Anything can be **enchanted**
(the witchlight makes it glow when it sounds).

## The Heartsongs

A lock of gold could never hold the demon of greed. So at the Locking the archmages asked the peoples of Aldmark for
things they loved more than gold, and sang the Locking into nine of them. Then they hid each one in an ordinary chest,
among millions, so the Maw could never find them all. They're the rarest things in the realm, and they're **mythic**,
the red quality nothing else reaches.

- **Why you hear them.** Only a Keywright hears a Heartsong sing: the ward that's made of the same song lets it through.
  It sings at the gentlest shake, and nothing drowns it out or stills it, lodestone or not. It doesn't knock about like a
  thing: each sings its own song, nothing like coin or glass. No seller has ever heard one, so none is ever priced.
- **Why they're worth nothing.** To anyone without the Key-mark a Heartsong is junk: a music box that won't play, a
  cold stone, a tin bird. No merchant would give a penny for one. The Guild won't take one in pawn: greed can't put a
  price on it.
- **Why they turn up anywhere, and again.** Let one go and it goes back to the Locking, to turn up in some other chest
  some day: a millpond chest as easily as the King's. About one run in three, in any chest a seller brings; the Locking
  keeps its verses apart, so each one you keep makes the next harder to find.
- **Why they won't stay in a chest you can't buy.** A Heartsong knows a Keywright. Pass on a chest you can't afford
  with one singing in it, and the chest bursts and lets it out to you: it alone, the seller's goods go home with them.
- **Why you keep them.** On display (no shelf needed: one hangs from the awning at Bram's stall), each one does something
  for the shop, the way the thing itself would.
- **Why they matter.** Bram's kin remember every one they've opened. The Crown Chest holds the Locking's tune; the
  Heartsongs hold its verses. With all nine known, Mara doesn't renew the Locking: she finishes it.

| Heartsong | Given by | Its song | On display |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Lullaby Box | a farmer's wife at Brindle: the music box that sang her children to sleep | a music box's lullaby | it calms every seller: nobody hurries you or minds what you do with their chest, and everyone asks a fifth less |
| The Shepherd's Pipe | a shepherd of the Brindle hills: the pipe that called any flock home | a breathy run on a pipe | it calls folk to your door: two more customers every day, and anyone who walks out comes back |
| The Fey Chimes | the fair folk of the Old Wood: the chimes over their door between the worlds | glass on a scale no bell is tuned to | fey luck: a third of what comes out as promised comes out a step better |
| The Dragon's Egg | Ysolt, the last dragon of the Ember Peaks: her egg, still warm | a heartbeat | dragon-gold: coin and gems sell for twice their worth |
| The Clockwork Lark | a tinker of Crownhold: the bird he built for a princess who never woke | birdsong and a ticking spring | it carries word across Aldmark: two more commissions a week, each paying half again |
| The Hearthstone | the dwarves of Ironroot: a stone from their first hearth | a warm, low hum | folk come for miles to sit by it, and pay at the door for every point of exhibition |
| The Drowned Bell | old Aldwater, before the river took it: the chapel bell that rang the hours | a deep, muffled toll | it still counts: it tolls once for every thing in a chest as it comes to the counter, false bottom and all |

## The sellers

changes what you can do or what the deal is, a place their chests come from, and a story they tell about it. What they
say is always true; what they miss is the opening. Some tell you something more, and it's always worth working out:
Pell's pawn ticket, Wenna's game scale, and the puzzles that point at a **warded thing** (below): Mother Bramble's
dreams, Gerda's dowries, Wren's packing tags, Lady Corisande's riddles, Quill's faded ward-rolls, Skrae's echoes and
Madame Oriel's cards.

## Why the rules are true here

- *A thing only sounds once the shake stirs it; clanks drown out delicate things.* The ward lets sound through and
  nothing else; things inside move and sound the way they would in any box.
- *Sellers price only what they think is there.* Nobody but a Keywright has ever seen inside one.
- *Locks.* The ward lifts once the chest is yours; the old iron lock underneath doesn't. Pick it cleanly and nothing's
  scratched; force it and glass breaks.
- *False bottoms.* People hid their best things under a floor when the Wardens came round with the Locking: the
  hollow boom of a drop is the only way to know.
- *Enchantment.* Things the archmages enchanted before they were locked stay enchanted; the witchlight shows it.
- *The lodestone.* Iron can't move near it. Arms go quiet, and so does demon gold, which is iron under the gilt.
- *Straw.* Smugglers pack their chests in straw so nothing clinks at the Wardens' toll. It muffles any shake a strength:
  what a gentle shake would stir needs a firm one, and soft things (cloth, paper) never stir through it.
- *Weight and counts.* A thing weighs what it weighs and is one thing, wherever it's hidden: a scale or a pawn ticket
  counts the false bottom too.
- *Warded things.* Anyone who knows the old craft can ward one thing inside a chest, the way the Locking warded the
  chest: it makes no sound and weighs nothing, and it only comes out for someone who names it. A seller can't name it
  (they never priced it), so it goes with the chest; a Keywright who works out what it is and marks one more of its
  kind on the slip than they can hear gets it when the chest opens, and anyone else leaves it in the ward. Whoever
  warded it always leaves a sign: a dream, a dowry's custom, a packing tag, a riddle, a faded roll, an echo, a card.

## The Bookkeeping Engine

The Gilded Guild's assessors don't shake chests: in the Guild's counting-house a clockwork the size of a wardrobe, built
by Crownhold's tinkers, reads a chest set before it and writes out what's inside (not how good: the ward keeps that).
The Guild sells one to any Keywright whose purse could pay off everything they owe, for exactly that sum: it would
rather be paid twice. With it on the counter, every chest's contents are on the slip before you touch it, and a run
that's already won can be finished in an afternoon.
- *Curios.* Oddments from the Locking that bend a rule of the trade. Nim finds them; nobody asks where. Each was made
  for a trade (a gambler's token, a smuggler's hook, a fence's mark, an appraiser's seal), and things made for the same
  trade work together, the way a fence's mark and a jeweller's loupe do in any thieves' kitchen. Nim knows a collector
  when he sees one: he notices what you buy and brings more like it, and he keeps the rarest (a curio made for the
  greatest of a trade, the Truthglass or the Golden Tongue) for someone who's shown him they're in that trade. His stock
  grows as word of you gets round (the further you've got, the more he trusts you with), and the very rarest only come
  to him once Bram's kin have done that trade often enough for the story to reach him. Some aren't a trade's at all: a
  Guild promissory note from before the Locking, still good, which the Guild honours once (it honours its own paper,
  whoever holds it).
- *Word gets round.* Sellers talk. A shop known to sell high, or to have things come out better than their chest, or
  to have a name for a kind of thing, pays more for its chests: everyone who brings one has heard, and asks that much
  more. What nobody can hear of is your eye, your tongue and your luck on the day: what's enchanted or under a false
  bottom or in a ward, a perfect read, a hard bargain, a chest taken without a hand laid on it.
- *Sight unseen.* A seller will knock something off for a buyer who takes a chest on their word, without a shake: it
  spares them the haggling over what was heard. Lay a hand on it, and the price is the price.
- *Restorers.* Things kept three hundred years in a Locking chest come out tarnished, creased and dusty. A restorer
  cleans, mends and polishes what you buy, and some of it comes up better than the chest promised; sellers know the
  shop does it, and ask a little more.
- *The ledger.* Bram's kin keep one ledger between them: a regular met, a find made, a curio seen goes in once and stays
  in, whoever's minding the stall.
- *Heartsongs.* Things loved more than gold, sung into the Locking: a Keywright hears them through the ward because the
  ward is their song. Nobody else knows they're there, so they're never priced, and they're worth nothing to anyone but
  you.
- *Hidden things.* Nobody could see an enchantment glow under a false bottom or in a ward, and nobody puts a spell on
  what they hide: what's hidden is never enchanted. But what someone thought worth hiding is a little special, and
  fetches a third more.
- *Commissions.* A regular who wants a thing pays more for it than any merchant would: always a quarter over what it
  would sell for, at least.
- *Who's who.* Bram's kin keep a page in the ledger for everyone they've traded with; look at whoever's across the
  counter and you know which.
- *Bram's stash and his word.* Old Keywrights keep a little by, and the Guild still takes Bram's word, once.
- *A rotten chest.* The ward holds a lid shut for ever; it doesn't stop the wood rotting. Barrow damp gets into
  everything, and a chest from there stands about two seconds of shaking before it starts to come apart.
- *A goblin's fee.* Goblins charge for everything, including looking.
- *A wager, an heirloom, a devil's due.* An archmage who remembers the Locking likes to see the craft done well; a
  knight's grandfather's sword is his whatever chest it's in; a devil never makes a bargain he doesn't win something by.
- *The King's game.* Aldric IX needs a Keywright he can trust with the Crown Chest, so he tests them the only way that
  counts: name what's inside. He comes by one day in four or so, the Maw one day in ten.
- *The Maw's gaze.* Greed is catching. Take his coin, and everyone in the city wants more today.

## What happens in town

Two days a week something happens for a day (something small in the first week, while you find your feet), and the
ledger's calendar shows this week's and next week's, day by day (with the story's mornings, the days Nim comes by, and
the Guild's nights). Each has a reason anyone in town would give you:

- **Fair Day**: the county's in town. **Locking Day**: the Locking's anniversary, and everyone spends. **The Crown's
  Buyer**: the Crown stocks up on one kind. **A Storm**: only those with something worth the soaking come out.
- **A Foreclosure Sale**: the Guild has taken a manor for its tithe and wants the chests gone, cheap.
- **The Tourney**: knights from four counties, buying steel and cloaks.
- **A Royal Chest**, **The Stranger**, **A Barge Aground**: someone (or something) unusual comes to the counter.
- **A Day of Mourning**: the old Duke is buried, and nobody shakes a chest hard in a town in black.
- **The Town Crier**: Lord Harrow lost his family's heirloom to the Locking, and pays three times its price for one,
  today.
- **The Minstrels' Fair**: minstrels trade songs, and whoever has a box that hums brings it in.
- **The Sleepers' Moon**: the Old Wood's moon is full, and everyone who slept under it dreamt true, like Mother
  Bramble. **Wardens' Day**: the Wardens carried the Locking's chests; on their day folk ward a keepsake into a chest
  the old way and leave a riddle for it. **The Guild's Count**: the Guild tallies everything that changes hands.
- **The Guild's Assessor**: the Guild sends a man to sit in your corner and take its tenth.
- **A Witchlight Moon**: the old enchantments wake under it.
